1 · Scope statement

V1 is a local-first, offline PWA for one person combining three light modules — tasks, habits, quick-capture notes — under one statistics/review surface. The habit engine is the centerpiece and deliberately gentle: decay-based strength scores and monthly completion rates, never hard streak resets. No gamification, no timers, no integrations, no accounts, no server. All data lives in the browser; the data model must be sync-ready, but sync itself ships later.

Your note, folded in as binding constraints:

"V1 must run entirely offline in the browser. Sync can wait until the habit sticks. No gamification — I know myself."

6 · Risk table (honest edition)

RiskSeverityReality checkMitigation in scope
Browser storage is evictableHigh"Entirely offline in the browser" means IndexedDB. Clearing site data — or iOS evicting storage for rarely-used sites — silently destroys everything.Export/import file backup is a v1 must-have, not a nice-to-have; request the persistent-storage permission; install as PWA (installed apps are evicted far less).
Two devices, two datasets until syncMediumYou checked multi-device sync but deferred it; nothing bridges phone and laptop in v1.Named and accepted in §3. Sync-ready data model keeps the fix cheap.
Decay-score algorithm complexityMediumLoop's strength score interacts non-trivially with flexible frequencies ("3× a week" — which misses count?). Easy to get subtly wrong and demotivating if it feels unfair.Implement Loop's published exponential-moving-average approach against the scheduled-day sequence; unit-test against hand-computed cases before styling anything.
Review flow scope creepMedium"Guided review" is where productivity apps quietly grow journaling, mood tracking, and other fn-5 violations.Hard rule from fn-5: the review may only show data the app already has and ask for taps, never typing.
Stats look empty for weeksLowDay/week/month dashboards need history; month views are meaningless until ~week 4.Design empty states honestly ("3 weeks until this chart means something") rather than faking density.
Three modules is still a lot for a v1LowTasks + habits + notes + stats is the honest reading of your picks, but it's 4 screens of surface area.Selections already cut the fat (flat tasks, plain notes). If time pressure hits, the habit engine is the centerpiece; tasks and inbox degrade gracefully to minimal lists.
